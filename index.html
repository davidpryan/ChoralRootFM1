ChoralRoot for the SMK-37

Quixotic7's chord instrument on the M-Vave SMK-37, with the keyboard's 3.5 mm jack as a MIDI output: it plays an FM-1 running LOOM-8, or any synth, with no computer.

Version 0.29.2-trs-smk37 (5 November 2026): a fork of ChoralRoot 0.29.2. GPL-3.0. A community test build, not tested by ChoralRoot's maintainer.

Get it

Install 0.29.2-trs-smk37

From Chrome or Edge on a computer, over the USB cable: the installer picks your keyboard's model (SMK-37 Pro, Elite, MKE-P37 or Starrykey 37 Play), checks the package, backs a keyboard already on ChoralRoot up to a file, and finishes an interrupted install. Read what it says before you tick the box.

Try it first

The same firmware in your browser, on the SMK-37's panel: the keys and pads with the mouse or the computer keyboard, Web MIDI out. No keyboard needed.

Source

This fork on GitHub, GPL-3.0; SMK37.md has the controls, the TRS jack and what to test. ChoralRoot is Quixotic7's, the SMK-37 port and the jack's driver genkigenki's, the base firmware Felucca by Hügelton Instruments.

TRS MIDI OUT: the 3.5 mm jack plays an FM-1 on LOOM-8, or any synth

  • The keyboard's 3.5 mm jack is now a MIDI output. With Options > TRS MIDI Out On (the default) it carries what USB MIDI out carries, with or without a computer.
  • On the jack: the chord on the MIDI Perform channel (1), the bass on MIDI Bass (2), the wheels, the pedal, and the MIDI clock with start and stop when MIDI Clock is Out.
  • An FM-1 running LOOM-8 on the other end of a TRS cable plays the chord on its track 1 and the bass on its track 2 (its tracks listen on channels 1 to 8). Set its CLOCK to receive to follow the keyboard's tempo.
  • Options > TRS MIDI Out Off leaves the jack quiet; USB MIDI keeps working either way. The option is kept across power-off with the global save.
  • Everything else is ChoralRoot 0.29.2 on the SMK-37, as the 0.29-smk37 build: the same sounds, screens and controls, with your settings, sounds, loops and favourites stored as before.
  • A fork of Quixotic7's ChoralRoot (the TRS output was asked for in its issue 38). Community-tested, not tested by the maintainer; the jack has not yet been tried on a unit by this fork either: the UART setup is the stock firmware's as genkigenki's porting guide read it, and the same driver ships in Vector37. If the option's line on the keyboard reads 'UART not answering', please report it.
  • Use a type A 3.5 mm to MIDI adapter for a DIN synth; a plain TRS cable reaches an FM-1's MIDI IN jack. SysEx (backups, the sound editors' dumps) stays on USB.
  • Updating from 0.29-smk37 keeps your settings, sounds, loops and favourites; going back to it works too (it simply does not use the jack).

With an FM-1 running LOOM-8

A 3.5 mm TRS cable from the SMK-37's jack into the FM-1's MIDI IN jack (or a type A adapter to a MIDI DIN cable for another synth). LOOM-8's tracks listen on MIDI channels 1 to 8: with the defaults the chord plays track 1 (MIDI Perform, channel 1) and the bass plays track 2 (MIDI Bass, channel 2). The wheels, the pedal and velocity go with the notes.

Tempo: set Options > MIDI Clock to Out on the keyboard and LOOM-8's CLOCK to receive, and LOOM-8's sequencer follows ChoralRoot's tempo and the loop's start and stop. Or leave the clock off and let LOOM-8 keep its own time. Options > TRS MIDI Out turns the jack off and on; USB MIDI keeps working beside it.

Before you install

Have M-VAVE's stock package for your model and their updater, M-UPGRADE, at hand: that is the way back. Keep the USB cable connected until the keyboard has restarted. The jack's setup follows the stock firmware as the porting guide read it and has not been confirmed on a unit by this project: if the keyboard's Options > TRS MIDI Out line reads UART not answering, please report it. Installing other firmware is at your own risk.
